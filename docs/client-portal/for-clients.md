# A guide you can send to your client

*This page is written for the person reviewing the articles. Send them the link and they can read it on their own.*

Welcome. This is your portal. It is where you read the articles written for your brand, ask for anything you want changed, and give the final go-ahead before a piece goes out.

You need about five minutes to learn it, and then it stays out of your way.

## Signing in

Your account has already been created for you. You were sent an email address ending in `@portal.strategi.is` and a password.

Go to the portal address you were given, type both in, and press **Sign in**. There is no sign-up page: your login is made for you, and it is the only one your organisation needs.

If your organisation has one brand, you land on that brand. If it has several, you land on a list of them and pick one. Anything waiting on you sits at the top of that list.

To sign out, use the button at the top right of any page.

## Your tabs

Down the left side, each brand has these:

| Tab | What is on it |
|---|---|
| **Overview** | Where the brand stands, in three numbers, with your most recent articles |
| **Content Roadmap** | The topics planned for you, and how many are written |
| **Questions** | Things about your brand our research could not answer. Nothing here is required |
| **Blogs** | Every article, in three stages |
| **LinkedIn**, **Medium**, **Bluesky**, **X** | Short posts made from your articles, one tab each |
| **Reports** | Your monthly performance report |

If your organisation has more than one brand, a picker above these rows switches between them, and **All brands** takes you back to the list.

## The Blogs tab

This is where most of your time goes. It has three tabs across the top.

=== "Needs answers"

    Articles held on a question only you can answer. Each one takes a minute.

=== "Ready to post"

    Finished articles waiting for you to read and approve. A row tagged **In progress** is one the team is still working on, and it opens with a short note instead of an article.

=== "Approved"

    Everything you have signed off, plus anything already live on your site.

Each row shows a number, the title, when it was written, and where it stands. The number is the article's row on your roadmap, so you and the team are always talking about the same piece. Click any row to open it.

## Answering a question

Sometimes a draft needs a fact only you have: whether a figure is right, whether a source says what it seems to say, whether something can be claimed at all.

When that happens the article appears under **Needs answers**, tagged **Waiting on you**. Open it and you see the draft on one side and the questions on the other. The draft is there for context. It is not final.

Type an answer to each one and press **Send answers**. All of them need an answer before the button turns on, because a half-answered form does not unblock anything.

After that the article goes back to the team, and your answers stay on the page so you can see what you said.

!!! note "Nothing is lost while you think"
    An article waits as long as it needs to. It does not expire, and it does not go out without your answer.

## Answering questions about your brand

The **Questions** tab holds things we could not learn from your website: a price you never
published, how many people you can seat, which year an award was from.

Your answers go straight into the fact base every article for you is written against, so a figure
you confirm here is one we can state with confidence later, instead of writing that it varies.

**Nothing here is required, and nothing is waiting on it.** There is no submit button. Answer what
you can, skip what you cannot, and leave the rest for whoever on your team knows it. Your answers
save as you type, so you can close the tab and come back.

If you are no longer sure of an answer you gave, clear the box. A withdrawn answer is better for us
than one you are not confident about.

!!! note "This is not the same as the questions that hold an article"
    An article held under **Needs answers** is waiting on you and will not go out until you reply.
    The **Questions** tab is not: it makes future articles better, and no article is stuck behind it.

## Reading an article

Open anything under **Ready to post** that is not tagged **In progress**. You get the finished article, a note at the top telling you where it stands, and an **Approve** button.

While an article is with you, this page always shows the newest version. If you ask for a change and the team makes it, refreshing shows you the updated text. There is no second link and no new email.

## Asking for a change

You do not need to describe where something is. You point at it.

1. Select the text you want changed. Drag with a mouse, or press and drag on a phone or tablet.
2. A card opens beside your selection, quoting exactly what you picked.
3. Type what should change in the box. It says *What should change here?*
4. Press **Send to the team**.

Write it the way you would say it out loud. "This figure is wrong, it should be 40%" and "cut this sentence, we cannot claim that" both work. You are describing the change, not writing the replacement.

Your note goes to the team with that exact passage attached, so nobody has to guess what you meant.

!!! tip "Your typing is safe"
    Once you have started writing, selecting other text in the article does not wipe your note. **Cancel** clears it, Escape closes the card, and Cmd+Enter (Ctrl+Enter on Windows) sends it.

### Following your notes

Every note you file stays beside its passage with a line telling you where it stands:

- **With the team**, meaning it is on their list
- **Resolved by the team**, meaning the article has been changed
- **Reviewed, no change**, meaning they read it and decided to leave the text as it is

You can keep adding notes while earlier ones are open, up to ten at a time. If you reach ten, finish the round with the team before adding more.

## Approving

When you are happy with an article, press **Approve**. A dialog asks you to confirm.

!!! warning "Approving is final"
    Approving locks the article. Those become the exact words that go out, and nobody can change them afterwards, you or the team. You cannot add notes or ask for fixes once it is approved.

    If anything still needs attention, cancel and leave a note on the text instead.

Occasionally the team sends an updated version while you are reading. If that happens as you press **Approve**, the dialog tells you the article changed and your approval was not recorded. Press **Read the new version**, read it, and approve that one. This is a safeguard: it stops you signing off on words you never saw.

## What happens after you approve

Your article moves to the **Approved** tab and is locked. From there the team takes it live.

Once it is live, the tag changes to **Published** and the article stays in your portal so you can always read exactly what went out. Where your site gives back an address, a **View live article** button appears beside it so you can open the real page.

Your approval is what allows publishing to your own website. Nothing goes live on your domain before you have pressed that button.

## The social posts

The **LinkedIn**, **Medium**, **Bluesky** and **X** tabs hold short posts written from your articles. They are not copies. Each one is a fresh piece written for that platform, saying what the article says in the shape that platform reads best.

They work exactly like the articles, with two sub-tabs: **Ready to post** for the ones waiting on you, and **Approved** for the ones you are done with.

Open a post and you can:

- Select any part of it and ask for a change, the same way you do in an article
- Press **Approve** when you are happy with it
- Press **Post to LinkedIn** (or **Post to Medium**, **Post to Bluesky**, **Post to X**) to copy the text and open that platform's composer in a new tab, ready to paste

That last button is there whenever you want to post something yourself. Approving tells the team it is ready; posting is the act of putting it out.

## Your roadmap and your reports

**Content Roadmap** shows the topics planned for your brand and how many are written so far. **Preview roadmap** opens the full plan, month by month, so you can see exactly what is coming and what each piece is meant to answer. It is there to read, not to edit.

**Reports** holds your monthly performance report: where your brand is being mentioned by AI answer engines, your links, and how your site scores technically, with a plan of action. A month appears here once the team has shared it. A month that reads "Report not available yet" has not been shared.

## Quick answers

**Can somebody else on my team sign in?** Yes. It is one login for your whole organisation, so share it with whoever reviews the writing.

**Do I have to answer questions and approve in one sitting?** No. Nothing expires and nothing goes out without you.

**Can I undo an approval?** No. Approving locks the article on purpose. If something is wrong after that, tell your Strategi contact and they will sort it out.

**I left a note days ago and nothing has changed.** Notes are worked on by a person, not instantly. The line under your note tells you where it stands. If it still reads **With the team** after a while, ask your contact.

**An article vanished from Ready to post.** Check the **Approved** tab. Approving moves it there.
