# The Content Roadmap

A roadmap is the list of topics a brand plans to write. Every blog the factory produces starts as one row on that list, and the row is the brief: it tells the research agent what to look for and tells the writer what the piece has to answer.

You reach it inside a brand, from the left nav, under **Content Roadmap**. It sits above **Blogs** in that nav on purpose: with no roadmap there is nothing to pick and nothing to write.

Uploading or generating a roadmap starts no writing at all. It parses the sheet and stores it. Blogs only begin when you pick rows on the **Blogs** page's **New** tab, which is covered in [The Blogs tab](blogs.md).

## What one row tells the factory

A roadmap sheet is a CSV with at least five columns. The first row is always treated as the header and is skipped, so your topics start on row 2.

| Column | Name | What the factory does with it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Content Topic | The subject, and the article's headline. Read **by position**. |
| 2 | What the Piece Covers | The scope and the angle. Read **by position**. |
| 3 | Format | Passed to the writer as guidance, labelled with your header text. |
| 4 | Search Intent | Passed to the writer as guidance, labelled with your header text. |
| 5 | Target Prompts | The AI-search questions the piece must be cited for. Read **by position**. |

Columns 1, 2 and 5 are found by their position and nothing else. The engine does not read your header text to work out which column is the topic, so those three columns must never move.

Every other column is kept, not thrown away. It reaches the writer under the header you gave it, so a `Format` of `Comparison anchor` and a `Search Intent` of `Commercial` are real instructions about the shape of the piece and the frame of its language.

A sheet may be wider than five columns, and a roadmap the factory generates is: it writes six, with `Est. Monthly Volume` sixth. That column sits after the prompts precisely so columns 1, 2 and 5 keep their places, and it reaches the writer as guidance under its own header.

!!! warning "Position matters more than the header name"
    If **Target Prompts** is not the fifth column, the writer is handed whatever text is there instead, and nothing warns you. A sheet with fewer than five columns is refused on upload, with a message naming how many columns were found.

### A worked example row

Here is one complete row, as it sits in the file:

```csv
"Content Topic","What the Piece Covers","Format","Search Intent","Target Prompts"
"Coworking desks in Indiranagar: what a 20 person team actually pays","Breaks down real monthly per desk pricing bands in Indiranagar, what is bundled at each band, what a private cabin adds, and the lock in terms teams get caught by. Carries a comparison table by band and links to the offer.","Cost breakdown","Commercial","which coworking spaces in Bengaluru suit a 20 person team | how much does a private office for 20 people cost in Indiranagar | is a managed office cheaper than coworking for a 20 person team"
```

Read as a table, the same row is:

| Column | Value |
|---|---|
| Content Topic | Coworking desks in Indiranagar: what a 20 person team actually pays |
| What the Piece Covers | Breaks down real monthly per desk pricing bands, what is bundled at each band, what a private cabin adds, and the lock in terms teams get caught by. |
| Format | Cost breakdown |
| Search Intent | Commercial |
| Target Prompts | three prompts in one cell, separated as described below |

### Target Prompts holds several prompts in one cell

One cell carries all the prompts for that row. The engine splits that cell two ways and accepts both:

=== "Newlines"

    Put each prompt on its own line inside one double-quoted cell. This is what a hand-written or exported sheet usually looks like.

    ```csv
    "which coworking spaces in Bengaluru suit a 20 person team
    how much does a private office for 20 people cost in Indiranagar
    is a managed office cheaper than coworking for a 20 person team"
    ```

=== "Space pipe space"

    Join them with a space, a pipe, then a space. This is what a roadmap generated by the factory writes.

    ```csv
    "prompt one | prompt two | prompt three"
    ```

Both separators work in the same cell, and surrounding quotes on each line are stripped. Splitting on newlines alone once made a generated cell parse as one long run on prompt, which is why both are accepted.

### What makes a row incomplete

A row is incomplete when it is missing its topic, what it covers, or its target prompts. Those three are the only required fields.

An incomplete row still shows in the sheet and still counts on the tab, under the **Incomplete rows** tile, which names exactly which fields are missing. You cannot select it for writing, and the engine refuses it at the API boundary rather than twenty minutes into a run.

A blank Format or a blank volume column never makes a row incomplete. That is a sheet that did not plan one.

## Two ways to get a roadmap

A brand with no roadmap shows an empty card offering both doors. A brand that already has one shows both in the toolbar at the top right, because each adds the next month rather than replacing anything.

### Upload a sheet you already have

Press **Upload roadmap** on the empty state, or **Upload month** in the toolbar. You can click to choose a file or drop a `.csv` onto the target.

The file has to be a `.csv`, and the limit is 2 MB. A file with any other extension is refused in the browser before it uploads. Exports from Excel and Google Sheets are read correctly, including files that carry a byte order mark.

An upload is refused when the file is empty, when it has a header row and no data rows under it, or when it has fewer than five columns. Every refusal from the engine comes back as the engine's own sentence, naming what was wrong.

### Generate one with AI instead

Press **Add New Month Roadmap**. One agent session researches the brand, its market and its competitors, then writes the sheet.

The dialog asks for two things:

- **Pieces**: how many topics to plan, 1 to 50. The default is 10, which is what the prompt's intent mix is written for: 6 commercial, 3 informational, 1 entity FAQ.
- **Notes**: optional, and binding when given. Use it for a geography lock, must-include topics, exclusions, named competitors, a different intent mix, or campaign context. It overrides the prompt's defaults.

There is no website field. The session always researches the brand's own website as recorded on its brand record, and the dialog names that website so you can see which one it will read. If the brand has no `http://` or `https://` website on file, the dialog says so in an amber note and the **Add New Month Roadmap** button inside it stays disabled until you add the website in the brand's settings.

!!! danger "This press costs real money and real quota"
    It starts one long research session against live Firecrawl and DataForSEO data, and it spends your Claude subscription quota. The dialog states this immediately above the button. The session runs in the engine, so refreshing the page, closing the tab, or opening it somewhere else does not stop it and does not lose it.

While it runs, the tab shows a card reading **Generating a content roadmap** with an elapsed clock. There is no progress bar and no percentage anywhere on this job, and that is deliberate: it is one session making an unknown number of tool calls, so the clock is the only honest number. The brand's **Overview** carries the same running line, with a button reading **Open the Content Roadmap tab**, so you do not press generate a second time.

When it lands, the card reads **Roadmap generated** and carries **What the agent reported**: what it pulled, what it cut, which uploaded resources it could read, what yielded no text, and any row it refused to plan because the brand's canonical facts forbid the claim. Read it. That report is often worth more than the sheet.

If the session breaks instead, the card turns red and reads **The roadmap generation failed**, with the engine's own sentence about what broke. The report, where there is one, is still underneath it.

The card also echoes **Researched from** and **Asked for**, taken from the engine's own record rather than from your form, so a job started in another tab still tells you what it actually ran with. Where notes were given, **Notes** sits beside them with a **View notes** button. Dismiss the card with the X once you have read it.

!!! info "The agent may deliver fewer topics than you asked for"
    It is told to write nothing rather than guess when it cannot research honestly. If it wrote fewer rows than you asked for, the card says so and the report says why. If it wrote nothing at all, there is no roadmap and the report is your answer.

Earlier months are handed to the session as a hard exclusion list, so a new month plans new topics instead of re-proposing what month 1 already covered.

## A brand holds many monthly roadmaps

Each upload or generation files the next month: Month 1, then Month 2, and so on. Nothing replaces an existing sheet.

Month numbers are never reused. If you delete Month 2, Month 3 stays Month 3 and the next one you add becomes Month 4.

The **Content Roadmap** tab always describes the latest month. Every tile on it counts that month alone, which is why a month one day old can read ten topics with ten remaining to write. Earlier months live on the [Blogs tab](blogs.md) behind its month picker, and inside the preview dialog.

## Preview: reading the sheet as it sits on disk

Press **Preview roadmap**. A sidebar lists every month the brand holds, newest first, each with its topic count and when it last changed. The newest is selected when the dialog opens.

The grid shows every column you wrote, at full contrast, with each column's role printed under its header: Topic, Scope, Target prompts, or Guidance. Nothing is greyed out, because nothing in the sheet is ignored. Under the grid you get the filename, its size, and when it was uploaded.

The preview fetches only when you open it, so visiting the tab costs nothing extra.

## Rewriting rows you do not like

The preview is also where you reject topics. Tick the rows that miss, say what is wrong, and one research session replaces exactly those rows.

The tick column is only on the latest month. Older months are history and are read-only.

1. Open **Preview roadmap** and tick the rows you want replaced. Shift-click ticks a range.
2. A bar appears under the grid asking **What is wrong with these topics (rows 3, 7)**.
3. Write the note. The button stays disabled until you do, because the correction is the whole point of the rewrite.
4. Press **Rewrite 2 topics**.

One note covers the whole batch. Address a single row by its number, as in "Row 3: drop the comparison framing", and that instruction lands on that row alone. Anything unscoped applies to every ticked row.

Every unticked row stays word for word, and the total never changes. The engine splices exactly one new row per rejected row and refuses the whole batch otherwise, leaving the sheet untouched. The pre-rewrite sheet is archived first.

You can start another batch while one runs, as long as the rows do not overlap. A running batch owns its rows: its checkboxes become spinners, the rows wash amber, and the **Preview roadmap** button on the tab reads "Preview roadmap (rewriting 2)" so you can see work in flight with the dialog closed.

Each batch shows its own line: **Rewriting rows 3, 7** with an elapsed clock, then **Replaced rows 3, 7** or **The rewrite of rows 3, 7 failed** with the engine's own sentence. Press **Report** to read the agent's account of what it planned, then dismiss the line with the X.

!!! note "A row with a blog on disk cannot be rewritten"
    Any blog locks its row, at any status: shipped, waiting on an answer, failed, stopped, or generating right now. The blog was written from that row's brief and is joined to it, so replacing the row would orphan the article. Hovering the checkbox tells you which case it is, and where the way out is deleting the blog first, the sentence says so.

Blank lines in the sheet cannot be ticked either. There is no select-all: rejecting every topic is not a rewrite, it is a sheet worth deleting and generating again.

## Downloading a roadmap

**Download** on the toolbar gives you the latest month. The download icon beside each month in the preview sidebar gives you that month.

Both offer two formats, because they are for two different people:

- **Excel (.xlsx)**: formatted, to show a client. It carries a title row naming the brand and the month, a styled header, wrapped cells and column widths.
- **CSV**: plain, to edit and re-upload. The uploader accepts `.csv` only, so this is the round-trippable copy.

Files are named `<brand-slug>-month-<n>-roadmap.xlsx` or `.csv`, and both are built from the sheet as it stands right now.

## Deleting a month

Deleting is per month, from the trash icon beside that month in the preview sidebar. There is no delete button on the toolbar, because a brand no longer holds only one roadmap.

!!! danger "Deleting a month deletes that month's blogs with it"
    Every blog written from that month's roadmap goes: its draft, its evaluation, its research and its history. That includes blogs already sent to a client or published on their site. This cannot be undone.

    A post already live on a client's own website is **not** taken down. Only this app's record of it goes.

    The sheet itself is archived first, so the topic list can be recovered. The blogs cannot.

The confirm names the month and its topic count, and lists all of the above before you press. The buttons are **Keep it** and **Delete Month 3 Roadmap**.

The app never edits your sheet. It can add a month, and it can delete one whole. It never rewrites a cell or writes a status back into your file.

## Why the tab locks while a run is live

While any blog run is live for the brand, the tab shows an amber card reading **A run is live for** the brand, and uploading, generating, rewriting and deleting are all refused.

The reason is the same in every case: the running session's topics came from this sheet and it is still reading them. Changing the sheet underneath a run would leave the status table describing rows that are no longer the ones running.

Two more locks work the same way, and each says its reason on screen rather than letting you discover it from a refusal:

- A generation is already running for this brand, so a second one is refused. The running one is the card on the same page.
- Topic rewrites are still running, so a new month is refused until they land.

Everything frees up on its own the moment the work finishes.

## What the tiles mean

The tab's tiles all describe the latest month.

| Tile | What it counts |
|---|---|
| Topics on the roadmap | Every row planned this month. |
| Blogs written | This month's blogs on disk, at any status. It can exceed the topic count, from blogs made off the roadmap. |
| Shipped | Scored 90 or above on the first evaluation with nothing left to ask. |
| Waiting on you | Held on a question only a person can answer. A blog is held at any score, a 96 included. |
| Below bar | The loop ran, scored the draft, and it missed 90. There is an article to read, and sending it is your call. |
| Did not finish | The run reached no verdict: the session died, stopped responding, or was refused before it opened. Generate the topic again. |
| Stopped | You pressed stop while these were in flight. Nothing was deleted. |
| Remaining to write | Topics with no blog on disk yet. |
| Running | In flight right now. |
| Incomplete rows | Rows missing topic, covers or prompts, naming which. |

**Did not finish**, **Stopped**, **Running** and **Incomplete rows** appear only when the number is above zero. For what each of those states means for the blog itself, see [What each status means](../concepts/blog-states.md).

Under the tiles, **What these blogs are about** counts the terms that recur across the topics, with the number of topics carrying each. It is counted from the sheet, never summarised by a model. A roadmap of unrelated topics honestly reports no recurring theme.

## Who can do what

Anyone signed in can open the tab, preview any month, and download. Uploading, generating, rewriting and deleting are admin only, and the engine refuses them from a non-admin account.

Your client sees a read-only version of the same page in their portal: the **Topics on the roadmap**, **Shipped** and **Waiting on you** tiles, the theme card, and a **Preview roadmap** with the same grid. They get no download, no delete, and none of the engine outcome tiles.
