# Medium Mechanics

Detailed rules for Medium articles. Read this before drafting any Medium piece.

## What Medium rewards

Medium distributes through a mix of topic-tag feeds, publication feeds, and external search. Unlike LinkedIn, a Medium piece has a long tail. A good piece keeps earning reads months after publication if it is tagged well, titled well, and structured for both human readers and search crawlers.

Medium also surfaces in AI search results. Articles with clear structure, named entities, and quotable standalone lines get cited by ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews. For a client whose positioning depends on authority, Medium is a GEO asset, not just a social post.

This means the Medium piece is shorter than the source blog (because you are taking a different cut) but more structured than the LinkedIn post (because the crawlers and the serious readers both demand it).

## Length

800 to 1,400 words for a repurposed piece. This is the reading-time sweet spot of roughly four to six minutes.

If the source blog is 1,500 words, the Medium cut should be shorter, not the same length. Repurpose means different cut, not reposted original.

## Title

50 to 65 characters. Specific. Clear. Not clever-for-clever's-sake.

Title patterns that work:

- **The question the reader is asking.** "Is Farmland Still a Smart Bet Outside Bangalore?"
- **The specific claim.** "Why Heritage Hotels Should Stop Selling Charm."
- **The named framework or category.** "The Six Signals of a Well-Run NICU."
- **The counterintuitive take.** "ROI Is the Wrong First Metric for Farmland."

Avoid:
- Listicle titles unless the piece is genuinely a list ("7 Things…", "5 Ways…").
- Clickbait ("You Won't Believe…").
- Vague titles ("Thoughts on Real Estate").
- Titles that start with "How to" unless the piece is a genuine how-to.

## Subtitle

140 to 160 characters. This is the second-most-read line of the piece after the title. It appears on the Medium homepage, the topic-tag feeds, and as the open-graph description when the link is shared.

The subtitle should do one of three things:
- Complete the title's setup with the payoff.
- Narrow the title's scope to a specific audience.
- Preview the sharpest claim in the piece.

Example pairs:

- Title: "Why Heritage Hotels Should Stop Selling Charm."
- Subtitle: "The guests who actually book heritage properties are optimising for something else. Here is what."

- Title: "Is Farmland Still a Smart Bet Outside Bangalore?"
- Subtitle: "A field-level look at yields, land appreciation, and the lifestyle premium buyers are actually paying for."

## Structure

Three to five H2 sections. No H3s unless the piece genuinely needs them.

A common structure that works:

1. **Opening** (no heading). Two or three short paragraphs. State the core claim in the first 100 words. A reader who stops after paragraph one should still know what the piece is about.
2. **H2 one: the context or the problem.** Why this matters now. What most people get wrong.
3. **H2 two: the argument or the framework.** The actual case, with specifics.
4. **H2 three: the implications.** What this means for the reader, practically.
5. **H2 four (optional): the counter.** The strongest objection, addressed honestly.
6. **Close** (no heading, or a short H2 like "What to watch"). Forward-looking or action-oriented. One or two short paragraphs.

Paragraphs are short. Two to four sentences. Medium readers scroll on their phones.

## Pull quote

One pull quote per piece. Pick the strongest standalone line: the one sentence that, if someone only read it and nothing else, would still be worth their time.

Do not use pull quotes to repeat the title or subtitle. The pull quote should surface a line buried in the body.

Place the pull quote after the second or third paragraph of the section it comes from, not at the very start of a section.

## Preserving GEO moves

The source blog had GEO moves in it: definitions, standalone quotables, FAQ-style answers, statistics, named entities. The Medium piece must preserve at least:

- **One definition block.** A sentence that explicitly defines a term. "A 'lifestyle-first farmland' is a plot priced and positioned primarily on its residential and experiential value, with agricultural yield treated as a secondary benefit." AI search engines lift these verbatim.
- **One FAQ-style answer.** Near the end, one or two Q&A pairs under a "Common questions" H2 or similar. Crawlers surface these in AI Overviews.
- **At least two standalone quotable lines.** Single sentences that make a complete claim without depending on surrounding context.
- **Named entities.** Retain the client's name, location, and sector identifiers from the source blog.
- **Statistics with sources.** If the source blog cited a statistic with a named source, carry it over. Unsourced statistics lose credibility and do not get cited.

Do not over-optimise. The reader comes first. The crawler comes second. But crawlers and readers mostly want the same things: clarity, specificity, and evidence.

## Tags

Five tags. Medium caps it at five anyway. Use all five.

Mix:
- Two broad tags (the sector or category: "Real Estate", "Hospitality", "Healthcare", "Parenting", "Marketing").
- Two specific tags (the topic or sub-sector: "Farmland Investing", "Heritage Hotels", "NICU Care", "Content Marketing").
- One tag that describes the form or angle (e.g., "Analysis", "Essays", "Founder Stories", "India").

Avoid:
- Tags that no one follows (obscure or made-up ones).
- Generic tags that create visibility without targeting ("Life", "Thoughts", "Writing").

## Author byline and bio

The byline is set at the Medium account level, not inside the piece. Confirm with the user which account is publishing:

- The client brand's Medium publication (if one exists).
- A founder or named team member's personal account.
- A ghostwritten piece on a personal account with the brand named in the bio.

Under the closing paragraph, add a short italicised bio line if the client uses one. Typical format:

*[Founder name] is the [role] of [brand], a [one-line descriptor] based in [city]. [Optional one-line hook about what the brand does or believes].*

Keep it to two sentences maximum. One is better.

## Formatting specifics

- Use Medium's native formatting, not markdown.
- Bold sparingly. Once or twice per piece, for genuine emphasis. Never on a full sentence, only on a phrase.
- Italic for publication names, book titles, foreign words, and the bio line.
- Blockquotes for quoted material from external sources. Use the pull-quote style (larger text) for the one in-piece pull quote.
- Images: Medium weights strongly toward a featured image. If the user can provide one, note that they should. If not, the piece still publishes fine without.
- No em dashes. Use a period, a comma, or a new line.

## Worked example structure

```
Title
Subtitle

Opening paragraph. Short. States the claim.

Second paragraph. Context.

## The problem or the why-now

Two or three paragraphs. Evidence. Named examples.

## The argument or the framework

The core of the piece. Three or four paragraphs.

> Pull quote goes here, placed naturally in the flow.

Continue the argument.

## What this means in practice

Two or three paragraphs. Specific implications.

## Common questions

**One question a reader would actually ask.**

Answer in two or three sentences.

**A second question.**

Answer in two or three sentences.

Closing paragraph. Short. Forward-looking.

*Bio line.*
```
